A Corrected Metric Does Not Validate a Vocabulary Scaling Law

Re-expressing the 60 released runs of Tao et al. (2024) in bits per character moves their 70B vocabulary prescription from 212K to 106K–119K or 550K–764K, depending on the fitting approach. Byte-pair encoding can lose more than half of the optimal compression

Kozma and Voderholzer showed that byte-pair encoding recovers between 1/3 and 5/8 of the optimal pair-encoding utility in the worst case. An explicit family of strings lowers the upper bound to (1−e−2)/2 ≈ 0.4323, and against encodings whose tokens have length at most three that constant is exact.

Is δ small? Measuring the premise of a hallucination lower bound on OLMo-2

Kalai, Nachum, Vempala and Zhang argue that pretraining keeps the slack term δ of their hallucination bound small, but do not measure it. On their birthday example, with OLMo-2's public corpus for the weights, the 7B base model gives δ = +0.028 (95% CI [0.017, 0.042]); the result is fragile to the weighting, and the floor is vacuous on this frame.

AdaGrad under heavy-tailed noise: symmetry, asymmetry and damping

Liu asked whether AdaGrad's threshold p = 4/3 under heavy-tailed noise is real. It is, and it is caused by asymmetry: with symmetric noise the rate is T−(p−1)/(2p) for every p, while with general noise and small damping (3p−4)/(4p) is sharp for every step size. Damping closes the gap, linearly in α wherever the exponent is positive.

Long documents make your batch smaller than it looks

Chunks of one code repository or paper give correlated gradients out to 16K tokens. At equal tokens, a whole-document 16K block has 3.5–4.9× fewer effective independent samples than packed web text on code and about 2.3× fewer on arXiv, measured with 512-token chunks on Pythia and OLMo-2 checkpoints; books stay close to the control.
